CENTRAL INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY AND SCIENCES
ACADEMIC REGULATIONS

Chapter: Examination Eligibility, Absence, Internal Assessment, Backlog,
Grading, Academic Probation, Governance Committees, Graduation,
Credit Requirements, Internship and Project Requirements

This document forms part of the official Rulebook of the Institute and is
binding on all registered students. It supplements, and where indicated,
provides specific exemptions relevant to, the Attendance Policy, Fee
Payment and Deadlines, Scholarship Policy, Hostel Handbook, and Student

8. EXAMINATION ELIGIBILITY AND ABSENCE

8.1 General Eligibility Confirmation
The Controller of Examinations publishes a provisional eligibility list for
each semester examination at least two weeks before the examination
period begins, based on attendance records submitted by course
instructors and confirmation from the Accounts Office that the
Examination Fee has been paid. A student appearing on the provisional
list as ineligible may seek correction of a factual error, such as a
misrecorded attendance figure, by application to the Controller of
Examinations within five days of publication.

8.2 Medical Exemption to the Attendance Threshold
Notwithstanding the general attendance requirement, a student who has
completed all internal assessment components in a course and who has an
approved medical certificate on file for the period of absence may appear
for the semester examination in that course with an attendance record as
low as 60%, provided the certificate is submitted within seven days of
the student resuming classes after the medical absence. The medical
certificate must be issued by a registered medical practitioner and must
state the specific dates of incapacity.

8.3 Procedure for Claiming Medical Exemption
An application for medical exemption under Section 8.2 is made to the
Head of Department, accompanied by the medical certificate. The Head of
Department forwards approved applications to the Controller of
Examinations for adjustment of the eligibility list.

8.4 Examination Absence Due to Medical Emergency
A student who is unable to appear for a scheduled semester examination
due to a medical emergency, supported by a medical certificate issued
within 48 hours of the missed examination, may apply to the Controller of
Examinations for the examination to be treated as a Medical Absence. A
Medical Absence entitles the student to appear in the Special Examination
described in Section 8.5, without payment of the Re-examination Fee
described in Section 14.2 of the Fee Payment policy.

8.5 Special Examination for Medical Absence
The Institute conducts a Special Examination approximately four weeks
after the end of the regular examination period, open only to students
whose absence has been confirmed as a Medical Absence under Section 8.4.
This Rulebook does not extend the Special Examination, or any equivalent
arrangement, to absence arising from personal, family, or social
commitments that are not medical in nature; no provision in this
Rulebook addresses examination absence caused by attendance at a family
function such as a wedding.

9. INTERNAL ASSESSMENT

9.1 Components of Internal Assessment
Internal Assessment for each course consists of class tests, assignments,
and, where applicable, laboratory evaluation, in proportions determined
by the course instructor within limits set by the Department Committee,
and published to students at the start of the semester.

9.2 Weightage
Internal Assessment carries a weightage of 30% of the total marks for a
theory course and 50% for a laboratory course, with the semester
examination carrying the balance.

9.3 Internal Assessment Absence
A student who misses a scheduled Internal Assessment component due to a
Medical Absence, verified under the same standard as Section 8.4, is
permitted a make-up assessment scheduled by the course instructor within
two weeks of the missed component. No make-up assessment is offered for
an Internal Assessment component missed for reasons other than a verified
Medical Absence; the student's score for that component is recorded as
zero.

10. BACKLOG AND RE-EXAMINATION

10.1 Definition of Backlog
A course in which a student has failed to obtain a passing grade, or in
which the student was ineligible to appear for the semester examination
under Section 8.1, is recorded as a Backlog course.

10.2 Re-Examination
A student with a Backlog course may appear for a Re-Examination in that
course at the next occasion the course's semester examination is
conducted, upon payment of the Re-examination Fee described in Section
14.2 of the Fee Payment policy, and subject to re-registering for the
course's Internal Assessment component if the original Internal
Assessment score is more than two academic years old.

10.3 Maximum Attempts
A student may attempt the semester examination for a given course,
including all Re-Examinations, a maximum of four times. A student who has
not obtained a passing grade after four attempts is referred to the
Academic Committee, which may permit re-registration for the course as a
fresh enrolment or may require the student to withdraw from the
programme.

11. GRADING

11.1 Grading Scale
Grades are awarded on a ten-point scale: O (10), A+ (9), A (8), B+ (7),
B (6), C (5), P (4), F (0). A grade of P or above is a passing grade for
undergraduate courses; a grade of C or above is a passing grade for
postgraduate courses.

11.2 Grade Change Requests
A student who believes a grade has been recorded incorrectly due to a
clerical or computational error may request a grade change by written
application to the course instructor, countersigned by the Head of
Department, within fifteen days of the grade being published. Grade
change requests may be made only by the student concerned; requests made
by a parent, guardian, or other third party on the student's behalf are
not entertained and will not be processed.

11.3 Retaking a Passed Course
A student may not re-register for, or retake the semester examination
in, a course in which a passing grade has already been recorded, for the
purpose of improving that grade. The recorded grade for a passed course
is final once published, subject only to correction under Section 11.2.

12. ACADEMIC PROBATION

12.1 Imposition of Academic Probation
A student whose semester grade point average falls below 5.0, or who
accumulates more than two Backlog courses in a single academic year, is
placed on Academic Probation for the following semester by decision of
the Department Committee.

12.2 Conditions of Academic Probation
A student on Academic Probation is restricted to a reduced course load
not exceeding 18 credits, is required to meet with an academic advisor
at least twice during the semester, and is subject to the scholarship
consequences described in Section 15.3 of the Scholarship Policy.

12.3 Continuation of Existing Scholarship During Probation
A student placed on Academic Probation retains any scholarship already
awarded and disbursed for the current academic year for the remainder of
that academic year; imposition of Academic Probation affects only new
scholarship applications, which are suspended for the duration of the
probation period, and does not affect an existing award already in
payment.

12.4 Exit from Academic Probation
A student exits Academic Probation upon achieving a semester grade point
average of 5.0 or above and reducing outstanding Backlog courses to two
or fewer, as certified by the Department Committee at the end of the
probationary semester.

13. ACADEMIC COMMITTEE AND DEPARTMENT COMMITTEE

13.1 Composition and Powers of the Academic Committee
The Academic Committee consists of the Dean of Academics (Chair), two
Heads of Department nominated annually by the Academic Council, and the
Controller of Examinations. The Academic Committee may waive attendance
requirements, examination eligibility conditions, or other procedural
requirements of this Rulebook in exceptional circumstances presented to
it in writing, and its decision on any such waiver request is final.

13.2 Meetings
The Academic Committee meets at least once per semester and additionally
as required to hear appeals under Section 19.3 of the Student Conduct
regulations and waiver requests under Section 13.1.

13.3 Department Committee
Each Department Committee consists of the Head of Department (Chair) and
two senior faculty members nominated by the Head of Department. The
Department Committee hears first-level academic grievances, including
attendance disputes under Section 3.5 of the Attendance Policy and
imposes Academic Probation under Section 12.1.

14. GRADUATION REQUIREMENTS AND CREDIT REQUIREMENTS

14.1 Minimum Credit Requirement
A student must complete a minimum of 160 credits, including all
compulsory core courses prescribed for the programme, to be eligible for
graduation in an undergraduate programme. Postgraduate programmes require
a minimum of 64 credits.

14.2 Minimum CGPA for Graduation
A student must achieve a final CGPA of at least 5.0 to be awarded the
degree. A student who completes the required credits but does not meet
this CGPA threshold may re-attempt Backlog courses, subject to the
maximum attempts rule in Section 10.3, to raise their CGPA above the
threshold.

14.3 Time Limit for Completion
An undergraduate programme of four years' standard duration must be
completed within seven years of first registration; a postgraduate
programme of two years' standard duration must be completed within four
years of first registration. A student who does not complete the
programme within this time limit is withdrawn from the programme.

15. INTERNSHIP AND PROJECT REQUIREMENTS

15.1 Mandatory Internship
Every undergraduate student must complete a minimum of eight weeks of
internship with an organisation approved by the Department's Internship
Coordinator, ordinarily undertaken between the sixth and seventh
semesters, and must submit an internship report evaluated by a faculty
supervisor for a grade recorded on the transcript.

15.2 Final Year Project
Every undergraduate student must complete a Final Year Project spanning
two semesters, supervised by a faculty member, culminating in a written
report and an oral defence before a panel constituted by the Department
Committee.

15.3 Group Projects
A Final Year Project may be undertaken individually or in a group of up
to three students, with individual contribution assessed separately by
the supervisor for the purpose of grading.

16. POSTGRADUATE RESEARCH AND PUBLICATION REQUIREMENTS

16.1 Thesis Requirement
Every postgraduate student must submit a thesis embodying original work
carried out under the supervision of a faculty member, in addition to the
coursework requirements of the programme. The thesis is evaluated by two
examiners, at least one of whom is external to the Institute, followed by
a viva voce examination.

16.2 Publication Requirement
A postgraduate student in a research-intensive programme, as designated
by the Department Committee at the start of the programme, must submit
at least one manuscript arising from the thesis work to a peer-reviewed
journal or conference before the thesis is submitted for evaluation.
Proof of submission, not acceptance, is sufficient to satisfy this
requirement.

16.3 Extension of Thesis Submission
A postgraduate student who is unable to submit the thesis within the
standard programme duration may apply to the Department Committee for an
extension of up to one additional year, renewable once with the approval
of the Academic Committee, upon payment of a continuation fee determined
annually by the Accounts Office.

16.4 Plagiarism Check
Every thesis must be accompanied by a similarity report generated by the
Institute's approved plagiarism-detection software. A thesis with a
similarity index above 15%, excluding quotations, references, and the
bibliography, is returned to the student for revision before it may be
sent for examiner evaluation.

17. AMENDMENT AND INTERPRETATION

17.1 Amendment of these Regulations
These Academic Regulations, and the associated Rulebook, Attendance
Policy, Fee Payment and Deadlines, Scholarship Policy, Hostel Handbook,
and Student Conduct documents, may be amended from time to time by
resolution of the Academic Council. An amendment takes effect from the
date specified in the resolution, or if no date is specified, from the
start of the next academic year, and does not apply retrospectively to
matters already finally decided before that date.

17.2 Interpretation and Precedence
Where a provision in one of the documents listed in Section 17.1
conflicts with a provision in another, the Registrar shall refer the
conflict to the Academic Committee for a binding interpretation. Pending
such a referral and decision, no student shall be presumed to be
bound by the stricter of the two conflicting provisions, nor entitled to
rely solely on the more lenient of the two; the matter is treated as
unresolved until the Academic Committee issues its interpretation.

17.3 Language
The English-language version of every policy document issued by the
Institute is the authoritative version for all purposes.
