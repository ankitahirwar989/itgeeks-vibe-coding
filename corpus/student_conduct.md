# Student Conduct, Discipline, and Appeals
## Central Institute of Technology and Sciences

---

## 17. Student Conduct

### 17.1 General Standard of Conduct

Every student is expected to behave with honesty and integrity in all academic and non-academic activities, and to treat members of the Institute community, including staff, faculty, and fellow students, with respect.

### 17.2 Academic Dishonesty

Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to: plagiarism in assignments, projects, or theses; unauthorised collaboration on individually assessed work; use of unauthorised material or devices during an examination; impersonation of another student in an examination; and fabrication or falsification of data in laboratory or project work.

### 17.3 Ragging

Ragging, defined as any act by a senior student intended to intimidate, harass, or embarrass a junior student, in any form, on or off campus, is strictly prohibited and is treated as a serious disciplinary matter carrying penalties up to and including expulsion, in accordance with applicable law.

### 17.4 Use of Institute Property and Systems

Students must not use Institute computing systems, network, or email for unlawful purposes, for harassment of any person, or for commercial activity not authorised by the Institute.

## 18. Disciplinary Procedures

### 18.1 Reporting

A complaint regarding student misconduct may be filed by any member of the Institute community with the Dean of Student Affairs or, for academic dishonesty, with the concerned course instructor or Head of Department.

### 18.2 Investigation

On receipt of a complaint, the Dean of Student Affairs (or the Head of Department, for academic dishonesty complaints) constitutes a fact-finding inquiry and provides the student with a written statement of the allegation and an opportunity to respond in writing within seven days.

### 18.3 Levels of Penalty

Penalties for proven misconduct range, in order of increasing severity: (a) written warning; (b) fine; (c) academic penalty, such as cancellation of the assessment concerned; (d) disciplinary probation for a defined period; (e) suspension for a defined period; (f) expulsion from the Institute.

### 18.4 Determining Severity

The severity of penalty imposed is determined by the Head of Department (for academic dishonesty) or the Dean of Student Affairs (for all other matters), having regard to the nature of the offence, whether it is a repeat offence, and any mitigating circumstances raised by the student. Penalties at levels (d), (e), and (f) require confirmation by the Academic Committee before taking effect.

### 18.5 Disciplinary Probation

A student on disciplinary probation under Section 18.3(d) remains enrolled but is subject to conditions set by the Dean of Student Affairs, which may include restricted access to hostel or campus facilities, mandatory counselling, or a probationary period during which any further finding of misconduct results in automatic escalation to suspension.

## 19. Appeals

### 19.1 Right of Appeal

A student subject to a disciplinary penalty under Section 18.3, or an adverse academic decision such as denial of examination eligibility or a grade dispute, has the right to appeal.

### 19.2 First Appeal to the Department Committee

An appeal against a decision made at the department level, including attendance disputes referred under Section 3.5 of the Attendance Policy and academic dishonesty penalties under Section 18, is first made in writing to the Department Committee within fifteen days of the decision being communicated.

### 19.3 Second Appeal to the Academic Committee

A student dissatisfied with the decision of the Department Committee may appeal to the Academic Committee within fifteen days of the Department Committee's decision being communicated. The decision of the Academic Committee on such an appeal is final and binding on the Institute and the student, and is not subject to further appeal to the Director or any other officer of the Institute.

### 19.4 Appeals Against Dean of Student Affairs Decisions

A disciplinary decision made directly by the Dean of Student Affairs under Section 18.4 (other than a hostel disciplinary decision, which is governed by Section 16.9 of the Hostel Handbook) may be appealed to the Academic Committee within fifteen days.

### 19.5 Form of Appeal

Every appeal must be submitted in writing, must state the grounds of appeal, and must be accompanied by any documentary evidence the student wishes the appellate body to consider. An appeal that does not state specific grounds may be summarily dismissed without a hearing.

## 21. Student Grievance Redress Cell

### 21.1 Constitution

The Institute constitutes a Student Grievance Redress Cell, chaired by a senior faculty member nominated by the Director, with two student representatives (one undergraduate, one postgraduate) elected annually, and one external member drawn from outside the Institute.

### 21.2 Scope of the Grievance Redress Cell

The Grievance Redress Cell handles grievances relating to harassment (other than ragging, which is governed exclusively by Section 17.3 and reported directly to the Anti-Ragging Committee), discrimination, and administrative delay that do not fall within the specific appeal mechanisms described in Sections 19.2 to 19.4. The Grievance Redress Cell does not have jurisdiction to overturn an academic decision or disciplinary penalty for which a specific appeal route exists elsewhere in this Rulebook.

### 21.3 Filing a Grievance

A grievance may be filed in writing or through the online grievance portal at any time. The Cell acknowledges receipt within three working days and aims to resolve straightforward administrative grievances within thirty days; more complex matters, including those requiring an inquiry, may take longer, and the Cell will communicate an expected timeline to the complainant.

### 21.4 Confidentiality

The identity of a complainant before the Grievance Redress Cell is kept confidential to the extent possible consistent with a fair inquiry, and no student is penalised for filing a grievance in good faith.
